## Voice in one sentence

Plain-English, declarative, requirement-driven engineering register — confident builders writing for other builders, with quiet mission-grade ambition ("Fix education, everywhere.") sitting just under a no-fluff surface.

## Tonal register (with scale anchor)

On the scale **McKinsey-formal → Stripe-precise → Linear-minimal → Vercel-energetic → indie-hacker-casual → Discord-meme**, SuperBuilders lands between **Stripe-precise and Linear-minimal**, with a Gauntlet-influenced lean toward elite-operator framing.

- The partner brief reads almost exactly like a Stripe API spec: numbered requirements, "must / must not" modal verbs, no adjectives that aren't load-bearing.
- The public web copy is even more minimal than Linear: one sentence per idea, no hero animations in the copy, no marketing adjective stacks.
- Job postings push slightly toward the Gauntlet-AI "elite operator" register ("high-agency," "Own end-to-end," "ship") — still restrained, never bro-y, never memey.

## Style signatures

Confirmed from observed text:
- **Sentence case headings** on the marketing site ("Fix education, everywhere.") — but **Title Case** in the partner brief ("Required Pilot Scope," "Browser-First Computer Vision Inference"). The brief is more formal.
- **Hyphens for compound modifiers**, not em-dashes: "browser-based," "pass/fail," "research-grade," "controlled production pilot," "AI-native," "AI-first," "full-stack," "rule-based." This is extremely consistent.
- **Oxford comma**: present. "...the prompt, camera preview, attempt state, result, hint, and retry or next action."
- **Contractions**: rare-to-absent in the brief and on the .school site (formal); present on LinkedIn ("Hi, we are...") and X (informal).
- **Exclamation marks**: zero on the .school site and zero in the partner brief. Used on Alpha School (sibling brand) and occasionally by founders on X, but not by the SuperBuilders org voice itself.
- **Numbers**: written numerically — "75-100 beginner vocabulary items," "ASL 1," "10-week immersive cohort" (Gauntlet), "2 hours" (Alpha). They do not write "ten-fold" or "10×."
- **Slashes**: comfortable — "pass/fail," "edu/acc," "teacher/administrator," "camera/video."
- **Imperative + must**: the dominant brief sentence shape is `[Subject] must [verb] [object].` Example: "The application must support American Sign Language only."
- **Negative requirements stated explicitly**: every section pairs what the pilot does with what it explicitly does not do. "The pilot must not attempt full sentence recognition, conversational ASL interpretation, open-ended signing, or phrase-level translation."

## What they DON'T do

- **No mascot voice.** No "Hey there!", no "Let's get signing!", no anthropomorphized assistant character.
- **No exclamation marks** in org-level copy.
- **No emoji** in their org-level copy (founders' personal X posts are out of scope).
- **No marketing adjective stacks** ("powerful, intuitive, beautiful, fast"). Adjectives are load-bearing only.
- **No aspirational pull-quotes** from learners or partners on the .school site.
- **No "research-grade" or "classroom-assessment-grade" claims** — they explicitly state when something is **not** that grade. The brief: "The pilot is not required to be classroom-assessment-grade, research-grade, or reliable across all real-world environments."
- **No infantilizing tone toward learners.** The brief calls them "the learner," not "the user" and not "the student." Neutral, respectful.
- **No "fellows."** They are GauntletAI graduates, but SuperBuilders calls them **builders**.

## Translating to our ASL app microcopy

The brief calls the person using the app **"the learner"** — never "user," never "you" in instructional copy. The brief calls hints **"targeted hints"** tied to **"handshape, movement, location, orientation, timing, or camera framing."** Use that exact taxonomy in our hint strings. The brief explicitly objects to one phrasing: do not say only "incorrect" — be specific.

Concrete rewrites in SuperBuilders register (left = generic edtech, right = on-brand):

1. "Great job! You nailed it!" → **"Pass."** (single word; matches the brief's "pass/fail result" language)
2. "Oops! Try again 🙁" → **"Not yet. Check your handshape and try again."** (specific, no emoji, no exclamation)
3. "Welcome back, learner!" → **"Welcome back. Ready for a practice session?"** (no exclamation, "practice session" is the brief's term)
4. "We need your camera to continue!" → **"This app needs camera access to evaluate your signs. Camera frames stay on this device."** (mirrors Requirement 4 + Requirement 13 privacy language)
5. "Awesome streak! Keep going!" → **"5 signs mastered. 70 to go."** (numeric, calm; matches brief's vocabulary-count framing)
6. "Sorry, something went wrong 😞" → **"The recognition model could not score this attempt. Re-frame and try again."** (names the failure mode, gives the action)
7. "You're a sign language superstar!" → **omit entirely.** Praise inflation is off-brand. Replace with progress: **"Today: 12 attempts, 9 passes."**
8. "Sign for: APPLE 🍎" → **"Sign the concept: apple"** (the brief says "the prompted concept"; lowercase the vocabulary item; no decorative emoji)
9. "Camera denied! Please allow access." → **"Camera access was denied. Re-enable camera permission in your browser to continue practicing."** (handles the brief's Requirement 4 "denied, unavailable, or technically unsupported" case explicitly)
10. "Login" / "Sign Up" buttons → **"Log in"** / **"Create account"** (sentence case, the brief uses "log in" as two words: "The learner logs into the web application")

Voice rules to enforce in code review:
- Modal verb for requirements: **"must"** for app-must-do statements, **"can"** for learner-can-do.
- Refer to the person as **"the learner"** in documentation; **"you"** is acceptable only in direct UI copy.
- Refer to attempts as **"attempts"** and outcomes as **"pass" / "fail"** — these are the brief's words.
- Use **"practice session"** and **"prompt"** as nouns. The learner does not "play"; they "practice."
- Privacy claims should mirror Requirement 13: **"Camera frames stay on this device during normal practice sessions."**
